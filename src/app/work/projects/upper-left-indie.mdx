## A music project built to send people elsewhere

Upper Left Indie is a one-person curation project for independent artists from Oregon, Washington, Idaho, Alaska, British Columbia, and the wider Northwest. It serves listeners looking for music, artists submitting work, and the curator maintaining the project.

I owned the redesign from product audit through production: strategy, information architecture, UX/UI, the responsive Figma system, frontend implementation, and integration with the product’s existing data and services.

Figma remained the visual and interaction source of truth. Production work translated those decisions into the existing Next.js application while preserving its artist data, Spotify playlists, Supabase-backed submissions and shows, Bandcamp ingestion, newsletter calls, email delivery, admin playlist tools, and Stripe checkout.

## The navigation understated the product

The original experience had more to offer than its hierarchy admitted. Listening, artist discovery, submissions, and support competed for attention, while shows and the archive were difficult to find. “Support” also carried two different meanings: money sent directly to musicians and money contributed to keep the curation project running.

The audit found concrete accessibility and state gaps as well. Some supporting copy fell below AA contrast, newsletter consent was preselected, focus treatment was incomplete, and the submission form did not explain its format requirements or document validation, loading, success, and error states.

These are findings from the repository and rendered product—not claims about user behavior. The work did not include interviews, analytics, usability testing, or conversion data, so the case study does not manufacture them after the fact.

## Four product principles

- **Send listeners onward.** A track is an introduction; the useful next step is the artist’s own page, store, or listening destination.
- **Put the curator in the interface.** The selection is made by one person, so the process and limits should be visible rather than dressed as anonymous editorial authority.
- **Name the two kinds of support.** Buying from an artist and funding the project are different actions, with different recipients and priorities.
- **Make the constraints explicit.** Regional eligibility, submission cost, required formats, response expectations, and selection practices should not be hidden in error messages or footer copy.

## Organizing around the next action

The shipped navigation reduces the primary routes to Listen, Artists, Shows, and About, with Submit music kept as a distinct action. Archive moves into the listening story instead of reading like discarded content. Project support remains available, but it is deliberately quieter than direct artist support.

This structure gives each task one name: listen to the current playlist, browse artists, find older selections, discover shows, understand the curator, submit music, buy from an artist, or support the project. The distinction matters more than adding another navigation item.

## From audit to a reusable system

The visual direction uses a warm page surface, narrow display type, straightforward body type, and restrained clay accents. The system documents semantic color, type, a four-pixel spacing base, and layout modes for 390, 834, and 1440-pixel frames.

Accessibility decisions are part of the foundations. Corrected text colors meet AA contrast against the page background; focus has its own high-contrast token; strong borders carry form affordance; and subtle borders remain decorative rather than the only signal.

## Artist discovery leads outside the platform

The production artist index reads the generated Bandcamp directory and derives region, primary genre, and recency from the data it actually has. Filters live in the URL, so a result can be shared and restored. Every filter remains at least 44 pixels tall, selection uses fill and border rather than color alone, and the result count is announced through a polite live region.

Cards normalize image geometry so a photo, artwork, or initials fallback never shifts the content. Production also cleans scraped biography artifacts and preserves the additional artist links already present in the dataset rather than dropping them to match a simplified mockup.

The more important change is where each card ends: a direct Bandcamp action. The portfolio piece does not treat time-on-site as the goal; discovery should continue at an artist-owned destination.

## Submission guidance before validation

The shipped form states the regional scope, that submission is free, what information is required, and what the curator can realistically promise. Helper text explains why a Spotify track link is useful instead of waiting for an error to reveal the rule.

Consent is separated into two choices. Permission to use public artist information is explicit; newsletter enrollment is optional and unchecked. Validation moves focus to an error summary, async outcomes are announced, and success becomes a confirmation panel with a consent recap.

The UI work did not replace the submission pipeline. A successful submit still saves to Supabase, sends email, optionally queues a Bandcamp artist page, and optionally calls the newsletter endpoint. Partial failures are handled so one unavailable integration does not erase a submission that another service already accepted.

## Supporting artists is not supporting the project

The support route separates two actions. Buying music or merchandise sends money to the artist; a project contribution covers the project. Direct artist support appears first and receives the stronger treatment.

The Figma file used illustrative operating-cost copy, so those unverified figures were not shipped as fact. Production preserves the existing one-time Stripe Checkout flow, maps each approved support option to its configured Price ID, and scopes loading and error feedback to the selected tier.

## Responsive behavior changes the composition

## Translating the design into the working product

The Figma screens used representative content to establish hierarchy. The implementation replaced it with the product’s real behavior:

- The stylized playlist panel became the real Spotify embed and active-playlist link.
- Recent track cards join Spotify playlist data to accepted submissions when a match exists, then link outward to Spotify and Bandcamp.
- The artist directory renders the generated Bandcamp dataset, including real artwork, locations, releases, and secondary links.
- Archive keeps the active and archived Spotify playlists distinct.
- Shows retains its Supabase read, server-side venue filter, revalidation, and configured and empty states.
- Submission keeps the Supabase, email, Bandcamp-queue, and newsletter calls behind the redesigned form.
- Project support keeps one-time Stripe Checkout rather than introducing a new payment model.

Two adaptations were deliberate. Archive and Shows had no dedicated high-fidelity frames, so I built them from the Figma foundations and shared components instead of inventing separate visual languages. Illustrative artist pairings, curator dates, playlist counts, and support-cost figures in the design were replaced or omitted whenever the running product could not verify them.

## Shipped outcome

Upper Left Indie now has a clearer public-route model, a reusable responsive UI system, real data behind its discovery surfaces, explicit interaction and form states, and a working distinction between supporting musicians and funding the project.

  src="/images/projects/upper-left-indie/upper-left-indie-production-submit.png"

The work ships without a numerical business claim. Useful follow-up measurement would include artist-destination clicks and submission completion, and usability testing would test whether the new route model matches how listeners and submitting artists actually move through the product.
