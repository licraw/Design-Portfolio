## What it is

The product gallery is the media experience on Fender product pages — the thing a customer scrolls, expands and rotates before deciding to buy a guitar.

Fender's product and design team owned the visual direction. Liam's work was engineering: turning that direction into a gallery that holds up in production, across brands, on real devices.

## What the interaction had to do

- **Sticky desktop layout.** The media column holds position while the buying details scroll beside it.
- **Masonry and carousel presentation.** The same media set reads as a composed grid on desktop and a swipeable carousel on touch.
- **Variant-driven media.** Changing a finish or configuration swaps the entire media set without the layout jumping or reloading.
- **360-degree media and modal.** Full-screen rotation of the instrument, with drag and keyboard control.
- **Loading and skeleton states.** Guitar photography is heavy; the layout reserves its space before the images arrive rather than reflowing around them.

## Why it was harder than it looks

A gallery is where a product page's hardest problems collect. Media counts vary per product. Variant switches change aspect ratios. Sticky positioning interacts badly with lazy-loaded images that change height after paint. Safari needed its own handling for sticky behavior and for the 360 viewer's pointer events.

The architecture had to survive all of that and stay reusable, because the same gallery runs across multiple brands and storefronts rather than being rebuilt per site.

This case study does not claim authorship of Fender's visual design. The design direction came from Fender's product and design team; the production interaction, component architecture, performance work and browser edge cases were Liam's.
