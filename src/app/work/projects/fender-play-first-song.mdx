## What it is

Fender Play's first-song onboarding is the sequence that takes someone who has just picked up a guitar to playing something recognizable. Fender's product and design team established the key states and the visual direction.

What a static handoff cannot specify is what happens between those states — how a step resolves into the next, what leads and what follows, what happens when a user moves faster than the animation. Liam built the production implementation and made those calls.

## Static design → interaction → production motion

The design defined where the experience starts and where it lands. The engineering work was everything in between:

- **Lottie** for the illustrated sequences the design team authored.
- **The Web Animations API** for the transitions between states, where timing needed to respond to what the user did rather than play a fixed clip.
- **Sequencing and timing** across both — deciding what overlaps, what waits, and how long a beat holds before it starts to feel slow.
- **Interruption behavior** — what a transition does when someone taps through it, which no static frame describes.

## Working with the design team

The decisions that weren't specified were resolved with the designers rather than around them: build the interaction, watch it, adjust the timing together. Credit for the product direction and the visual design belongs to Fender's design team; the motion behavior in the shipped experience was implemented and, where underspecified, decided in engineering.
